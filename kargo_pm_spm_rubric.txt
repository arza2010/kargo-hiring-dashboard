KARGO · PM / SPM HIRING RUBRIC
Version 0.2 · DRAFT · pending Arjun's confirmation (Human gate 0.5)

Sources: 8 past-hire CVs + ratings from the case brief; both JDs (PM, SPM).
Not yet reviewed: hires/ interview notes and outcome notes.
Changes in v0.2: C5 anchors rewritten against the JDs; Part F added
(JD cross-check); years and location handling defined in Part A.

Pattern name (proposed): "OPERATORS WHO BUILD"
Kargo's best hires personally did the operational work Kargo's customers do,
hit a gap in it, built the fix themselves, and got others to adopt it.
They owned the outcome with no layer between them and the user.


======================================================================
PART A · HOW TO SCORE
======================================================================

1. Score every CV on 5 criteria. Each criterion is scored 0–4 using the
   anchors below.

2. Criteria C1–C4 are the same for both roles. C5 is scored TWICE: once
   against the PM anchors and once against the SPM anchors.

3. Weighted score = sum of (weight × level ÷ 4).
   Output two totals per candidate: PM score /100 and SPM score /100.

4. EVIDENCE RULE: every level above 0 must quote the CV line that
   justifies it. No quote = level 0, marked "not evidenced — probe".

5. "Not evidenced" is NOT "evidence against". Keep them separate. A short
   or badly formatted CV should produce probes, not penalties.

6. One event may support two criteria only if it genuinely shows both.
   Otherwise count it once, under the stronger criterion.

7. Score the work described, not the wrapper. Ignore CV length, design,
   and the number of metrics quoted.

8. NEVER use or infer from: name, gender, age, photo, marital status,
   college name or tier, CGPA/marks, city or hometown, family background,
   employer brand prestige, or employment gaps (turn gaps into a probe).
   Certifications and tool lists score nothing unless tied to a described
   outcome.

9. The rubric ranks; it does not reject. No cut-off score. Every candidate
   stays visible to Arjun.

10. YEARS OF PM EXPERIENCE are NOT scored. The JDs ask for 2–4 (PM) and
    5–8 (SPM), but in the past hires more years did not mean better
    (Vikram had more PM years than Lavanya and rated lower). Instead,
    report "PM years (from CV)" as an info field and flag if it sits
    outside the JD range. Arjun decides what the flag means.

11. LOCATION is NOT scored and NOT sent to the AI. Both roles are
    in-office Mumbai (relocation accepted). This is a logistics question:
    the interview invite asks the candidate to confirm it.

PART B · THE FIVE CRITERIA
======================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------
C1 · OPERATOR-SIDE EXPERIENCE IN THE CUSTOMER'S WORLD      WEIGHT 30
----------------------------------------------------------------------
What it means
  Has personally done the operational work Kargo's customers do: moving
  shipments, handling documentation and customs, coordinating carriers,
  resolving exceptions. Knows the user's day from the inside, not from
  interviews.

Why it is weighted highest
  All 5 "Exceeds" hires have it. None of the 3 "Meets"/"Below" hires do.
  It is the only clean separator in the set.

Anchors
  4  1+ year hands-on in freight forwarding, customs brokerage, 3PL,
     port/terminal or carrier operations — shipments, documents or
     exceptions handled directly.
  3  Customer-facing or commercial role INSIDE a logistics operation,
     working alongside ops daily; OR hands-on ops in adjacent physical
     operations (warehousing, manufacturing supply chain, fulfilment floor).
  2  Built for, sold to, or integrated with logistics from the outside
     (e.g. API integrations with 3PLs) without doing the operational work.
  1  Studied the domain (user research, consulting, analysis) only.
  0  No evidence.

Look for
  Shipment volumes handled, document types named, counterparties named
  (CHAs, shipping lines, customs, carriers), exceptions resolved.

Guard
  Score the described work. Not the employer's name, not the city,
  not a family business connection.

Probe if moving forward
  "Walk me through a shipment that went wrong. What did you do in the
  first hour?"


----------------------------------------------------------------------
C2 · SELF-STARTED FIXES THAT OTHER PEOPLE ADOPTED           WEIGHT 20
----------------------------------------------------------------------
What it means
  Noticed a gap nobody assigned, built something to close it, and
  colleagues or other teams adopted it.

Why this weight
  All 5 "Exceeds" hires show the strong form. The others show weaker
  forms: builds that were part of their job, or no adoption evidence.
  It separates less sharply than C1.

Anchors
  4  Unprompted fix, born from a gap they hit personally, AND explicit
     adoption beyond themselves (team, other teams, became standard).
  3  Unprompted fix with adoption, but limited scope OR adoption only
     implied.
  2  Built something useful that was within the role's expected remit;
     OR unprompted but no adoption stated.
  1  Contributed to someone else's initiative.
  0  No evidence.

Look for
  "Found that…", "had no way to…", "built over a weekend", "adopted by",
  "now standard", "retained permanently".

Probe if moving forward
  "Who asked you to build that? What happened when people started
  using it?"


----------------------------------------------------------------------
C3 · OWNS THE OUTCOME WITH NO LAYER IN BETWEEN              WEIGHT 15
----------------------------------------------------------------------
What it means
  Worked end-to-end, directly with the user, customer or outcome,
  with no manager, PM or account layer buffering them. Took things to
  closure.

Why this weight
  5 of 5 "Exceeds" hires show it; 1 of 3 others (partially). Useful but
  not unique to the best hires.

Anchors
  4  Sole or senior-most owner AND in direct contact with the end user or
     customer; owned to closure ("sole", "no layer", "independently").
  3  Sole or senior owner without direct end-user contact; OR clear area
     ownership with a light layer above.
  2  Owns a component inside a larger layered team.
  1  Supporting role only.
  0  No evidence.

Probe if moving forward
  "Tell me about a call you made that nobody above you signed off on."


----------------------------------------------------------------------
C4 · DELIVERS UNDER CONSTRAINT                              WEIGHT 10
----------------------------------------------------------------------
What it means
  Kept things running through pressure without extra resources:
  volume spikes, vendor failures, deadlines, covering for others.

Why this weight
  4 of 5 "Exceeds" hires show it explicitly; the others only as routine
  job duty. It matters at a 40→70 person Series A, but it is the
  easiest criterion to overstate on a CV, so it is weighted lowest.

Anchors
  4  Specific incident: absorbed a spike, failure or gap with no added
     resources, and the outcome held.
  3  Delivered under a stated pressure or deadline, less detail.
  2  Pressure handled as a routine part of the role (on-call rotation).
  1  Generic claim only ("thrives under pressure").
  0  No evidence.

Probe if moving forward
  "What broke last quarter, and what did you drop to fix it?"


----------------------------------------------------------------------
C5 · PRODUCT JUDGMENT AT THE APPLIED LEVEL                  WEIGHT 25
     (scored twice: PM anchors and SPM anchors)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
What it means
  Evidence of product work at the level applied for, as each JD
  defines it. The two levels are different jobs, not the same job at
  different seniority:
    PM  = core operations platform; discovery with daily users;
          ship, kill, learn in short cycles.
    SPM = integration and data layer; long-consequence architectural
          calls (build / configure / stay away); senior-most PM who
          defines how the PM function works.

Why it is not weighted higher
  This is what the JD measures, and JD-matching is what failed for
  11 weeks. On shipping volume and credentials alone, Vikram ("Meets")
  looks as strong as Lavanya ("Exceeds"). The craft difference that did
  show is the one the PM JD names: she shipped AND killed things on
  evidence.

PM anchors  (from PM JD: "shipped things, killed things, and learned
            from both — preferably in short cycles")
  4  Owned a product area or roadmap end-to-end AND ran discovery
     directly with the people who use it daily AND shipped at least one
     thing and stopped/reversed at least one thing on evidence.
  3  Owned features spec-to-release with measured adoption, but no
     stop/reverse evidence, or discovery only through intermediaries.
  2  Contributed to product work (APM, supporting PM, spec-writing in a
     product-adjacent role).
  1  Product-adjacent exposure only.
  0  No evidence.

SPM anchors  (from SPM JD: integration/data layer; "calls in ambiguous
             situations and live with the consequences"; "help define
             the practices… the PM function will run on")
  Three SPM signals:
    (a) PLATFORM: owned an integration, data, API or platform area, or a
        product that had to work inside complex existing systems.
    (b) SENIOR CALLS: was the senior-most product decision-maker for that
        area (no senior PM above making the calls) and made
        long-consequence calls such as build vs configure vs avoid.
    (c) FUNCTION-BUILDING: defined practices, frameworks or standards
        that other PMs or teams adopted.
  4  All three signals, in an early-stage or rules-not-written setting.
  3  Two of the three signals.
  2  One of the three signals, OR a PM-level 4 record with none of them.
  1  PM-level 2–3 record with none of the signals.
  0  No evidence.
  Note: managing PMs is NOT required. There are no PMs at Kargo for the
  SPM to manage yet.

Probes if moving forward
  PM:  "What's something you shipped and then killed? How did you know?"
       "Tell me what a freight forwarder's morning looks like."
  SPM: "Tell me about an integration you chose NOT to build. What did
       it cost you, and would you make the same call now?"
       "What data-quality standard have you held a team to?"

PART D · LIMITS AND FLAGS FOR ARJUN (decide at Gate 0.5)
======================================================================

1. SMALL SAMPLE. 8 hires, only 2 PMs. The C1 split (5 of 5 vs 0 of 3)
   is striking but small. There is a plausible reason it is real: Kargo
   sells to freight forwarders, so people who have done the job
   understand the customer.

2. SEPARATES "EXCEEDS" FROM THE REST, NOT "MEETS" FROM "BELOW".
   Preetham (Below) scores above Rahul and Vikram (Meets). Whatever made
   Preetham "Below" is not in his CV. Check his interview and outcome
   notes in hires/.

3. "STILL AT KARGO AND THRIVING" NOT VERIFIED. Ratings are from the
   brief. Check the outcome notes for anyone who has left before
   treating them as a positive example.

4. C1 AT 30 COULD UNDER-RANK A STRONG PM WITH NO LOGISTICS BACKGROUND.
   Mitigated by: no cut-off, everyone visible, Arjun decides.
   If Arjun believes domain can be learned on the job, move 10 points
   from C1 to C5 (C1 = 20, C5 = 35).

5. INTERVIEW-ONLY TRAITS CANNOT BE SCORED FROM CVs. Example: Lavanya's
   "she doesn't hedge" came from a performance review. Traits like this
   go into the interview brief as probes, not into the score.

6. SPM ANCHORS ARE JD-DERIVED ONLY. No past hire was an SPM, so C5_SPM
   has no calibration. C1–C4 still apply, because the pattern held
   across every function, including engineering, sales and operations
   leads.

7. THE JD AND THE PATTERN DISAGREE ON ONE POINT. The PM JD asks for
   "genuine curiosity" about ground-level operations; the SPM JD calls
   ops familiarity "a genuine advantage". The past hires show that
   LIVED operational experience separates the best, not curiosity.
   C1 therefore scores lived experience, and curiosity becomes a probe.
   Arjun should confirm he agrees with this reading.
